# Older fielding history expands prospect follow up but needs a separate scale

The recovered 2009–2021 fielding records make substantially more early prospect
careers measurable. At the 2022 testing cutoff, the possible three-year training
population rises from 137–153 to 283–313 distinct people across the five held-player
folds. That is useful coverage, not an accuracy improvement: old UZR conversion
credit and native Statcast range cannot yet share a target.

The [source contract](defense-older-quality-v24-contract.md),
[precision amendment](defense-older-quality-v24-precision-amendment.md) and
[player review](defense-older-quality-v24-player-review.md) preserve the definitions
and qualifications. No learner, new skill label, forecast promotion, explorer
change or 2026 model selection occurred. The full defense goal remains active.

## What the source adds

Thirteen complete public, unqualified single-season tables contain 26,759 rows,
including pitchers, catchers and aggregate outfield listings. The separate 1B–RF
ledger has 15,826 player-position seasons. Of those, 14,566 have known range plus
error credit and independently acceptable official defensive exposure.

There are 15,811 exact official-outs matches. Twelve records differ by one to
three outs; three zero-out listings have no official counterpart. The same
existing absolute and relative exposure limits exclude three small stints:
Russell Mitchell and Trent Oeltjen in 2010 LF, and Chris Woodward in 2011 3B.
Their discrepancy is small in absolute terms but material to their tiny exposure.
Missing credit and zero innings never become measured average skill.

The original decimal-innings check stopped on legacy rounding, before a ledger
or model. The sibling repair retains exact baseball outs and the original
discrepancies; its small rounding allowance cannot hide a full defensive out.
It changes neither the exposure certification limits nor target eligibility.
The original reader, failed execution and captured response remain preserved.

Balls in zone, plays made and out-of-zone plays are present across the source
ledger, with nonnegative integer counts and plays no greater than balls in zone.
These are useful descriptive opportunities, not the full difficulty denominator
for UZR and not currently minor-league opportunities.
[FanGraphs RZR definitions](https://library.fangraphs.com/defense/rzr/).

## The additional prospect coverage

The original minor origins and three/five-year windows are unchanged. Before
2016, qualified older conversion measurements can supply *potential coverage*;
after 2016, only the existing qualified native range measurements count. The
audit never chooses the more favorable of overlapping metrics, computes a

Across the existing genuine-prospect origins, 191 additional distinct people

## Why the measurements cannot simply be joined

There are 6,742 certified same-year/same-position overlap rows in 2016–2021.
With at least 500 defensive innings in each measurement, 1,068 player-position
seasons from 428 people remain. The contemporaneous rate correlation is 0.550;
median absolute disagreement is 1.663 runs per 500 innings. Signs disagree in
369 of the 1,068 records. These are measurement comparisons, not forecast errors
or evidence that either source is ground truth.

The full retained populations expose a systematic reference difference. Native
CF centers are +1.061 to +1.772 runs per 500 innings in 2016–2021; native LF
centers are −0.986 to −1.766. Older conversion centers for those positions are
near zero. The selected substantial-exposure overlap has additional selection
effects and must not supply league centering constants.

This has a known baseball explanation: OAA originally compares outfielders across
positions, while UZR compares players with their own position. Starting-location
information and treatment of individual plays differ too. It is not evidence
that every center fielder improved or every left fielder declined.
[Statcast and UZR comparison](https://blogs.fangraphs.com/statcasts-outs-above-average-and-uzr/).

It also matters for player value. FanGraphs explicitly centers outfield range by
season and position before combining it with positional adjustments in WAR.
Raw native skill plus UZR-era position values is not automatically the same
accounting. The earlier research assembly is an explicitly expanded common-win
target, not published WAR; its skill gains are not withdrawn by this source
audit. Its conversion to a WAR-like player-value target needs this reference
check before any promotion.
[FanGraphs fielding update and correction](https://blogs.fangraphs.com/a-fangraphs-war-fielding-update/).

## Player findings and decision

Simmons has strong early measured conversion defense, whereas the three peers
nearest his minor age and exposure have unknown MLB defense, not zero talent.
Trout's 2009 CF record now has two measured MLB seasons by 2012, but three similar
minor peers have no MLB exposure within that window. Rojas's 2009 record still
has just one measured MLB SS season and 851 outs by 2014. No averaging rule should
turn that into a reliable five-year grade. Eduardo Escobar, selected without his
future results, qualifies only in the longer window.

The overlap walks preserve disagreements as well as agreement: Simmons's 2021
SS rate is −0.309 in older conversion and +5.644 in native range; Trout's 2016 CF
rates are +1.628 and −1.150. Castellanos subsequently moves from 3B to RF. His
outfield results are not missing or neutral third-base quality. Tiny one-inning
outfield stints produce extreme annualized rates; they are kept as observations,
not used as ready-made talent grades.

Keep the recovered source and its qualified exposure ledger. Do not splice it
into native labels or reopen failed count/DP weight sweeps. Next reconcile native
skill, position-relative fielding credit and the existing positional-value
assembly. Preserve intrinsic skill and value as separate outputs. After that,
contract either a qualified measurement bridge or an explicitly separate older
quality validation; more training coverage alone is not a bridge.
