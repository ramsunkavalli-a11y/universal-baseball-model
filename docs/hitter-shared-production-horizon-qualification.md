# Foreign and domestic profile horizons differ

2026-10-05. Inspection of the original foreign profile builder shows a design
qualification to the completed shared-production comparison. Its forecasts,
scores, seals and rejection remain unchanged; its interpretation is narrower.

The foreign `translated_probability` field is generated as a domestic
persistence intercept plus component slope times past foreign league-relative
production, plus an overseas offset. Its training outcomes are following-year
MLB counts. It therefore represents a predicted future distribution, not a
past-performance MLB equivalency. The domestic graph profile is instead a
same-season equivalency of observed past domestic events.

The shared-production runner pooled those two fields using observed PA before
adding a common prior. Reanchoring their references aligned coordinates but
did not align their temporal meaning or undo foreign regression. Consequently,
the test does not isolate a coherent common past-production representation.
Its original counts, arithmetic and empirical loss are correct, but its passing
source checks did not check this semantic invariant. The foreign subgroup gain
must not be interpreted as evidence that this heterogeneous pool is validated.

The earlier shared-event experiment explicitly handled this distinction by
forecasting domestic persistence before pooling future profiles. Its direct
conversion still lost and underpredicted homers. Repeating that conversion is
not the next experiment.

For the new count-likelihood design, foreign past inputs must come from the
archived own pooled probabilities and contemporaneous league references,
not the future `translated_probability`. Reconstruct them from the dated
counts and references, then estimate overseas context within the historical
future-event likelihood. League-relative foreign production is not already
MLB-equivalent; sparse training support must remain visible. All original
benchmarks and source additions remain, and 2026 is not reopened.
