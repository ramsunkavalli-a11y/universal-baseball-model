# Distinguishing surprising outcomes from systematic forecast flaws

2026-10-03. Apply the user's Belt correction to future model choices. Preserve
the original source records, contracts, scores and reviewed cases; this note
corrects the interpretation, not the historical forecast or his actual zero PA.

Belt's 2023 season supplied 404 MLB PA, 19 HR and 60 unintentional walks. The
fresher-ranking model gave him a 64.63% chance of appearing the next year and
377.77 PA if active, or 244.16 expected PA. His zero-PA outcome lies within a
forecast that also assigns 35.37% probability to no appearances. That fact alone
does not prove that the playing-time forecast was unreasonable. The user notes
that his failure to find a team was surprising. Do not turn that hindsight into
an automatic penalty for productive free agents or require the model to predict
each unusual market decision.

The descriptive group check selects by origin-known information: documented
current free agency, at least 200 recent MLB PA, and a positive fixed hitting
forecast. It includes 102 forecasts for 82 distinct people. The fresher-ranking
model expects 83.07 appearances versus 88 actual, and 37,299 PA versus 37,613.
There are fourteen zero-PA outcomes versus 18.93 expected absences. These are
pooled descriptive totals, not independent seasons or proof of calibration in
every subgroup. Both Belt seasons remain included. The already-completed
[employment player reviews](../reports/model-evidence/hitter-employment-v71/player-walkthrough.md)
trace Belt and Harper from raw source counts through the unchanged fresher-ranking
heads and failed peers. Those figures do not support a blanket extra penalty for
productive unsigned hitters. Good total allocation can still conceal individual
underpredictions and overpredictions.

Likewise, a star breakout that nobody could precisely foresee is not sufficient
reason for a name-specific boost. A miss becomes a useful model-design signal
when its source inputs or calculation are wrong, its probability is implausible
on information available then, or comparable players show a repeatable pattern.
Use actual origin-known stats and successful and unsuccessful peers; evaluate
the group's participation, conditional workload, expected PA and delivered
offense separately. Keep outliers in proper scores, but do not let one outcome
dictate a rule or erase uncertainty.

The employment addition remains unadopted for broader reasons: unsigned PA
error did not improve, aggregate/public gains were tiny and uncertain, intended
workload repairs did not materialize, and generic All-Star assignments cannot
be called contracts. Belt is not decisive evidence against higher estimates
for good unsigned hitters. His 2022-to-2023 miss and 2023-to-2024 exit serve as
opposite-risk examples, not a proof that either particular outcome was knowable.

The coherent next task is the repeatable thin-sample/readiness issue. The early
smooth prospect models were trained with older origin-year rankings; the newer
preseason source has now recovered information that materially affects arrivals.
Before reopening that approach, compare the exact old smooth opportunity model
against the same model with only the new ranking vintage. Do not tune its
penalties, change its feature scale, add college data or alter hitting. Keep the
old and newer tree anchors. This separates the effect of newly recovered source
information from the effect of a different model, rather than pretending earlier
negative tests on stale information settled the revised question.
