# Player examples for older defensive measurement and prospect coverage

These examples trace recorded fielding counts into measured MLB paths. They do
not compare two forecasts. Every prediction and accuracy-gain field is explicitly
empty. The fixed six 2015 MLB cases and three earliest prospect origins each
have three peers chosen from the same origin/position, using age then exposure
distance, without their later performance. The saved walks include all positions,
actual source splits, exposure qualification and annual measurements.

## Established players and component accounting

Older conversion credit is range plus errors. It excludes arms and double plays;
total UZR includes them. These are measured runs, not an estimate of peak ability.

| Fixed 2015 player | Position | Defensive outs | Range plus error runs | Runs per 500 innings | Total UZR |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Andrelton Simmons | SS | 3,838 | +12.887 | +5.037 | +16.799 |
| Mike Trout | CF | 4,088 | +4.096 | +1.503 | +4.736 |
| Nolan Arenado | 3B | 4,086 | +3.176 | +1.166 | +4.065 |
| Brandon Belt | 1B | 3,150 | +7.001 | +3.334 | +7.285 |
| Miguel Rojas | SS | 722 | +1.114 | +2.314 | +1.251 |
| Nick Castellanos | 3B | 3,527 | −6.560 | −2.790 | −5.406 |

Simmons's +7.122 range and +5.765 error runs produce +12.887 conversion runs;
another +3.912 DP runs explain the larger total UZR. Both older and native range
are strongly positive during much of 2016–2019. They sharply disagree in 2021:
−0.309 versus +5.644 runs per 500 SS innings. His peers Freddy Galvis, Jean Segura
and Didi Gregorius demonstrate the problem is not one anomalous player. Gregorius
has positive older and negative native SS rates in 2017 and 2018. Segura later
spends substantial time at second base; that does not certify his SS quality.

Trout's 2015 conversion credit excludes +0.640 arm runs. In 2016 the conversion
rate is +1.628, versus −1.150 native range, at the same CF exposure. In 2017 both
are negative. His peers Joc Pederson, Odúbel Herrera and Christian Yelich retain
their later corner-outfield moves. Yelich's single 2019 CF inning annualizes to
an absurd-looking +241.6 older runs per 500 innings; its actual credit is less
than one run. Correct annualization is not sufficient reliability for a forecast.

Arenado's peers are Matt Duffy, Jake Lamb and Hernán Pérez. Arenado is positive
in both overlapping measurements, but his 2016 rates differ markedly: +1.582
older versus +5.085 native. Lamb's 2016 older rate is negative while native is
positive. Duffy's later absence and Pérez's many positions stay in the path.
Reputation must not choose the preferred measurement or erase those cases.

Belt's first-base comparison uses Logan Morrison, Justin Bour and Paul
Goldschmidt. His 2015 +7.001 conversion runs are mostly range plus error credit,
not the same as native first-base receiving. His brief LF/RF appearances are
separate observations, not evidence for pooling those positions with 1B. Both
measurements weaken in 2016, but subsequent signs and magnitudes vary.

Rojas's 2015 SS record is only 240⅔ innings. His +2.314 annualized conversion
rate should not receive the confidence of Simmons's much larger exposure.
Marwin Gonzalez, Jedd Gyorko and Christian Colón are the origin-selected peers.
All later use several positions. Gyorko's small 2015 SS record does not license
calling his subsequent third-base quality a shortstop outcome.

Castellanos's 2015 3B conversion rate is negative. His peers are Kris Bryant,
Gio Urshela and Kaleb Cowart. Castellanos moves into RF in 2017 and primarily RF
thereafter; in 2018 both measurements are strongly negative at RF. That supports
keeping the position paths, not assigning later RF credit to a 3B label. Urshela's
2020 third-base rates disagree substantially despite matching exposure.

## Prospects and what longer follow up actually supplies

Trout's first eligible CF origin is 2009, age 17, with 1,096 defensive outs:
1,042 from combined rookie baseball and 54 from Single-A. His 70 putouts, one
assist and one error total 72 chances. The original source cannot call all of
this DSL or complex baseball. His later CF path has 323 outs in 2011 and 2,657
in 2012. Older credit supplies two measured seasons and 2,980 outs in the
three-year window; the five-year window supplies 9,780 outs across four seasons.
There is still no native grade for those pre-2016 seasons.

Jerico Blanco, Jose Monzon and Dioris Robles are his nearest-age/exposure peers.
Their current CF chances are 80, 89 and 85, with one, five and one errors. None
has MLB exposure within the five-year window. This is not proof that the players
were worse fielders: hitting, development and opportunity also affect arrival.
It prevents a survivor-only talent comparison from claiming population-wide
certainty from ordinary fielding totals.

Simmons's first eligible SS origin is 2010, age 20, with 1,597 outs, 101 putouts,
230 assists and nine errors: 340 chances. Four errors are recorded as throwing
errors and 47 double plays are credited. The three-year MLB SS path supplies
1,278 outs in 2012 and 4,057 in 2013, with positive older conversion measurements
in each. The five-year path adds 2014 and 2015 for 13,004 outs. These are real
previously missing measurements, not forecasts made from the nine-error record.

His peers Roberto Velasquez, Felix Gonzalez and Jorge Guzman also have recorded
SS exposure and errors, but no MLB exposure within five years. Gonzalez's origin
includes a small Short-A stint alongside combined rookie baseball. All original
levels and counts remain included rather than selecting whichever looks best.

Rojas's first eligible SS origin is 2009, age 20: 3,412 outs, 220 putouts,
340 assists and 13 errors, totaling 573 chances. Seven throwing errors and
72 double plays are recorded. He has no MLB exposure in the three-year window;
the five-year window has just 851 SS outs in 2014. Older conversion is positive,
but one season and fewer than 1,500 outs do not meet the existing quality rule.
His other 374 MLB outs are not silently converted to SS ability.

Eduardo Escobar, James Beresford and Carlos Martinez are Rojas's same-age peers.
Escobar's first three years supply only 195 SS outs, despite playing other
positions. By five years he has 3,160 SS outs across four seasons and possible
older-quality coverage. Beresford and Martinez have no MLB exposure within this
window. Rojas's later useful career remains outside this particular origin's
measurement horizon; an unknown label is not a negative talent judgment.

## Review conclusion

The source adds genuine older MLB observations, while the short-stint arithmetic,
metric disagreements, delayed arrivals and position changes explain why those
observations cannot be pasted into native skill targets. All nine peer groups
and selected raw counts/annual paths independently replay. The baseball review
is complete, not a predictive pass. Keep old and native measurement namespaces
separate and resolve position-relative value accounting before a bridge fit.
