# Shared production did not improve the hitter forecast

2026-10-05. The fixed historical comparison and
[ten player walks](hitter-shared-production-player-review.md) are complete.
Keep the existing model. The new shared event representation does not meet the
accuracy requirement, although its counts, reference alignment and sample
influence behave as designed. It is not a rejection of minor or foreign data.

## Matched historical result

Three talent branches were fitted in each of 35 chronological, held-player
cells. All 105 heads replay. The 30,506 original forecasts for 11,020 people
retain exactly the original playing time. Targets are 2017–2019 and 2022–2025;
there is no 2026 outcome access or new playing-time fit. Thirteen source additions
are separate, with no fabricated incumbent forecast.

Actual matched totals are 1,270,493 PA and 4185.43 batting-plus-replacement wins.
Hitting MSE worsens by 0.03162, nominal player-cluster interval 0.01480 to
0.04879. Delivered-value MSE worsens by 0.001302, interval 0.000411 to 0.002157.
These are exposed historical development intervals, not a pristine new test.
Hitting worsens in six of seven origin years; only origin 2023 improves.

The foreign-source original subgroup's hitting RMSE improves 1.88024 to
1.79782, but contains only 253 forecasts, 138 people and 7776 actual PA. It
does not outweigh the full-cohort loss or justify choosing that subgroup after
seeing results. Upper never-debut hitting worsens 2.56325 to 2.57826; lower
never-debut worsens 3.17908 to 3.28563. Lower-minors expected delivered value
increases 16.18 to 19.59 against only 9.48 actual. Non-arrivals' expected value
also rises 225.93 to 237.48, with actual zero. Near-correct overall totals conceal
these differences.

On the existing 2627 matched public rows, 2088 have actual MLB PA. Common-reference
hitting RMSE is incumbent 1.72848, shared 1.73019, archived Steamer 1.77459 and
ZiPS 1.75336. Release vintage, park and reference differences remain; this is
not proof that either UBM model is superior to the named public systems, nor
a comparison of certified ZiPS playing time or full WAR. The failed repair is
a secondary anchor; its delivered value also changes opportunity, so its value
comparison cannot isolate hitting.

## Baseball conclusions and next design question

Lee improves modestly; Yoshida worsens. Judge, Yordan and Kurtz all remain
underestimated and become worse. Perdomo is the largest gain but remains badly
low. Wilkerson's almost exact delivered value comes from opposing PA and hitting
errors, not accurate components. No-rate non-arrivals remain in value scoring.

All HR-versus-out partial contrasts are positive. The 95 K-versus-batted-out
alerts are reviewed, not automatically treated as physical errors; hits fixed
means implied BABIP changes. Exact source/coefficient accounting shows that
foreign and prospect production still acts mainly as a small residual beside
MLB quality, demographic and context controls. Sparse analogues and imperfect
translation are retained as limits.

Do not repeat a penalty sweep or the same representation with a favorable route
selected from these scores. The next substantive design question is whether
shared, translated production should establish a common batting baseline, with
MLB quality and tracking modeled consistently as refinements, rather than use
MLB residual relationships as the whole talent relationship where MLB quality
is absent. Before fitting, walk that proposed baseline's actual translated
values and older park/opponent evidence; do not assume that the translation or
an arbitrary prior is adequate. This remains within the coherent hitter goal,
not a new algorithm tournament or another job/school/return test.

Source gates and focused tests pass; predictive improvement fails; player
walkthrough is complete; adoption is not approved. Neither sealed forecast nor
the already completed one-time 2026 evaluation changed. The long hitter goal
remains active, and this milestone closes one substantive comparison.
