# Why the repaired head barely learns production evidence

2026-10-04. This is a no-fit diagnosis after the completed 41-player review,
not another experiment or a changed result. Saved weights, inputs and parameters
explain why the stable new forecasts do not prove the evidence is unhelpful.

## Evidence was reduced before the regression reduced it again

The repair multiplies minor and foreign production contrasts by their exposure
shares, then applies a Ridge penalty of 100. Smaller input magnitudes make the
same numerical penalty stronger relative to those signals. In a hypothetical
constant-share column, multiplying the input by r makes the equivalent penalty
on its raw coefficient 100 divided by r squared. At r=0.04, that is 62500 rather
than 100. Actual shares vary, so this is an explanation of the geometry, not a
single effective penalty for the fitted model or a measured stabilization point.

For every one of the 35 overseas talent heads, the weighted centered normal
equations reconstruct all fitted coefficients and the intercept. This independently
confirms the actual Ridge calculation. The source families were not accidentally
omitted or joined as zeros. The original features' future hitting training labels
also match the reconstructed future-relative target. There is no training-label
mismatch explaining this result; copied labels in older scored case tables are
a separate, previously corrected reporting problem.

Foreign production is available for only 7–31 distinct actual future participants
in the relevant training cells, accounting for 0.24–0.68% of normalized actual-PA
training weight. After attenuation and adjustment for the other regularized inputs,
the median design-information fraction against the 100 penalty is 0.000231;
the maximum is 0.01472. This diagnostic is not an exact multivariate shrinkage
factor against an unpenalized fit or a posterior reliability. It nevertheless
shows the penalty dominates these particular small, sparse contrasts. Exposure
flags have larger magnitudes and can dominate the apparent foreign effect.

Minor evidence has many more matching people, but much of that exposure belongs
to current MLB hitters' small prior minor or rehab samples. Its median corresponding
design fraction is only 0.01794, with substantial variation across events and cells.
Counts of people with any nonzero source are not counts of elite no-MLB prospects.
The regularization audit retains the actual per-cell people, weight fractions,
design information and coefficients; it does not recommend a tuned replacement.

## Removing a redundant coordinate also changes the penalty

The current translated profile uses eight probability deviations whose sum is
zero, including other outs. The repair uses seven deviations and omits other.
No probability information is lost by dropping one coordinate, but ordinary
Ridge penalties in those two coordinate systems are not equivalent.

For the old model, the equivalent seven-coordinate coefficient is each event's
coefficient minus the other coefficient. Kurtz's current HR contrast therefore
has equivalent coefficient 1.0383 on an unattenuated profile. The new profile
is attenuated to 4% before applying its newly fitted coefficient. Preserving
the number 100 alone does not preserve prior strength or effect. For a full
eight-coordinate symmetric penalty, the equivalent seven-coordinate penalty
matrix is I minus the all-ones matrix divided by eight, not I. This mathematical
distinction is separate from real changes to sources, context and routing.

These findings explain why the two repaired talent arms are nearly identical.
They do not show that reducing the penalty will predict better or that unrestricted
foreign coefficients are safe. The earlier amplified foreign fit remains bad.

## Coherent direction for the next design

Keep the useful current talent routing and the explicit new professional activity
sources. Do not replace the routed hitter model with another arbitrary pooled head.
Do not treat sample-share attenuation plus an inherited penalty as calibrated
Bayesian learning. Do not infer poor foreign or prospect talent from a default
zero contrast and a generic lack-of-MLB baseline.

The next contract should test shared event learning: translated minor and foreign
production should contribute to a common baseball-sized talent representation,
with explicit reliability and a coherent prior, rather than asking 7–31 foreign
participants to estimate seven isolated production relationships. Preserve newer
MLB evidence, draft/scout priors, stable rare-sample behavior and source uncertainty.
The representation must pass source-removal and coherent-event direction checks
before fitting. Any learning-strength choice needs chronological training-only
validation; do not choose it from Yordan's, Kurtz's or Suzuki's future errors.

Retain the original cohort and no-arrivals, separate availability from talent,
and require matched comparisons against the unchanged current model. Professional
activity is useful source evidence, not proof that the fitted new job model should
be adopted wholesale. Temporary restrictions still need a sensible availability
mechanism with genuine source support; hardcoding a famous player's realized return
is not a repair. No new fit is approved by this diagnosis alone.

The completed 2026 season remains unopened until a candidate is genuinely frozen.
The long-range goal is still active; this diagnosis narrows the next work instead
of restarting source collection, team-record testing or an algorithm tournament.
