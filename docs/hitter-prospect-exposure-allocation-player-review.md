# Prospect experience player review

2026-10-05. These walks diagnose unchanged one-calendar-year MLB forecasts.
They are not new fits. Historical rate/value outputs are the saved translated
prospect route; only the two playing-time heads are replayed here. That does
not newly validate the hitting model. Value means custom batting contribution,
not full WAR. All inputs come from the actual saved fitted input frame, not
older baseline columns preserved in prediction exports.

## Review and comparison rules

Seven historical players were fixed before this diagnosis. Three additional
historical cases are the largest cameo overestimate, largest cameo underestimate
and an ordinary cameo. Four exposed 2026 cases are explanatory only. For every
case the review retains stats, inputs, intermediate forecasts and reality.
Peers are selected without outcomes using the same origin, rank band, highest
and primary levels, age-known status and age within two years, followed by
distance on age, exposure, position and rank. Up to three are retained.

These peers are not complete talent, handedness, roster or position matches.
For example, Kwan's comparison set is not a group of equally good contact
hitters. It can expose contrary outcomes without establishing identical odds.
If strict rank/level matching leaves no peer, that gap is reported, not filled
with an unranked player. Training support counts distinct people in each actual
held-player training membership, with positive next-year PA required only for
the conditional head. Sparse counts are warnings, not precise probability
intervals or proof that a future success was foreseeable.

## Historical cases

**Nick Kurtz, origin 2024.** At age 21 he has 35 Single-A PA and 15 Double-A
PA, including four homers and ten walks in Single A. The actual preseason
input is listed with rank score .63; the old export's unlisted columns are not
his fitted inputs. The model uses that rank positively but forecasts a 6.1%
arrival probability and 168 conditional PA, yielding only 10 expected PA
against 489 actual. The hitting rate is 1.02 wins per 600 PA; contribution is
.05 versus 5.84 actual. Four matching training people exist but none arrived;
no same-origin strict peer exists. This is a severe miss of an unusual fast
mover, not proof that fifteen Double-A PA should look like a full season.

**Wyatt Langford, origin 2023.** Age 21, he has 106 High-A, 54 Double-A,
26 Triple-A and 14 rookie PA, with nine homers across High A and Double A.
His primary exposure is High A, not Triple A. The .95 rank score and draft
evidence push the replay upward. Probability is 59.9%, conditional PA 359
and expected PA 215, against 557 actual. Rate is 1.44 wins per 600; contribution
1.18 versus 1.80. Only two matching training people exist and neither was an
arrival. Strict peers Carson Williams and Jackson Holliday received zero and
208 PA, respectively. Langford needs stronger recognition, but those contrary
outcomes prevent treating this whole profile as a guaranteed full-time hitter.

**Julio Rodriguez, origin 2021.** Age 20, he has 206 Double-A PA with seven
homers, 37 strikeouts and 28 walks, plus 134 High-A PA. The missing 2020 minor
season is not poor performance; his 2019 stats remain in the source walk.
Roster membership and .98 rank are present. Probability is 80.5%, conditional
PA 316 and expected PA 254, versus 560 actual. Contribution is 1.31 versus
3.94. Eight matching training people include five arrivals. CJ Abrams, the
available strict same-origin peer, gets 85 predicted versus 302 actual PA.
This is underprediction with substantive upper-level evidence, not a cameo
label error. The already tested available-season correction helps some of
this era but is not a complete value solution.

**Ronald Acuna Jr., origin 2017.** Age 19, he has 243 PA at each of Double A
and Triple A, plus 126 High-A PA. Triple A wins the primary-level tie. The .99
rank and upper-level evidence are present, yet the forecast is 48.2% arrival,
211 conditional PA and 102 expected PA versus 487. Rate is .19 wins per 600;
contribution .34 versus 4.14. Two matching training people both arrived, with
no strict same-origin peer. His low result cannot be repaired merely by
restoring a substantial Triple-A sample that the model already received.

**Pete Alonso, origin 2018.** Age 23, he has 301 Triple-A PA with 21 homers
and 273 Double-A PA with fifteen homers. With rank score .50, arrival is
80.4% but conditional PA is only 268: expected PA is 215 versus 693 actual.
The rate is .59 wins per 600; contribution .87 versus 6.60. Eight matching
training people include seven arrivals. Strict peer Austin Riley is also
low, 141 versus 297 PA. Substantial exposure and power are in the source;
the future workload and batting breakout are both underestimated. Neither
was lost by an upper-minors reporting label.

**Jackson Holliday, origin 2023.** Age 19, primary exposure is High A at
259 PA; he also has 164 Double-A and 91 Triple-A PA. His rank score is 1.00.
The model forecasts 88.7% arrival, 396 conditional PA and 351 expected PA,
against 208 actual. Rate is .83 wins per 600; contribution 1.57 versus −.50.
His two narrowly matching training people have no active outcomes. Same-origin
strict peers Langford and Williams go in different directions, 557 and zero
PA. A universal elite-prospect workload/rate boost would worsen this case.

**Diego Cartaya, origin 2023.** Age 21, he has 403 Double-A PA with nineteen
homers, 117 strikeouts and 37 walks. His current preseason input is unlisted,
while an earlier rank score .87 remains available. Roster evidence strongly
raises arrival in the exact path replay. The forecast is 75.2%, 194 conditional
PA and 146 expected PA versus zero actual; rate −.82 and contribution .25
versus zero. There are 647 matching full-training people and 148 active people.
Strict peers Aliendo, Meidroth and Quero all have zero next-year PA. This is a
false high with adequate broad-profile support, not missing current experience.
The older rank's positive path is a mechanism worth noting, not proof that
rank retention itself caused the miss.

**Austin Meadows, origin 2016.** This is the largest cameo overestimate.
At age 21 he has 190 Double-A PA, 145 Triple-A PA and seventeen short-season
PA; his rank score is .91. Forecast arrival is 82.5%, conditional PA 361 and
expected PA 298 versus zero actual. Contribution is 1.05 versus zero.
Only three narrowly matching training people exist, all active. Strict peers
Brinson, Bellinger and Albies receive 55, 548 and 244 PA against forecasts of
283, 103 and 219. Similar upper-level/rank records do not guarantee the same
next-year role. This contradicts a one-directional workload remedy.

**Steven Kwan, origin 2021.** This is the largest cameo underestimate.
Age 23, he has 221 Double-A PA with 23 strikeouts and 22 walks, plus 120
Triple-A PA with eight strikeouts and fourteen walks. Contact evidence and
roster membership are present. Probability is 69.6%, conditional PA 167 and
expected PA 116 versus 638 actual. Rate −.25 and contribution .31 versus
3.35 are also low. There are 354 matching full-training people and 56 active
people. Gamboa, Cedrola and Leon are the mechanically selected peers and all
receive zero PA, but do not have equivalent contact/roster profiles. This
remains a missed skill/role combination; it is not evidence that every player
with 120 Triple-A PA should receive 600 MLB PA.

**Javy Guerra, origin 2017.** The ordinary cameo has 373 High-A and 145
Double-A PA at age 21, with heavy strikeouts at both levels. Probability is
26.6%, conditional PA 73 and expected PA 19.35 versus nineteen actual. Rate
−1.02 and contribution +.03 still differ from −.13 actual. There are 244
matching training people and 25 active people. Ockimey, Stokes and Tucker,
the selected peers, all have zero next-year PA. This case illustrates why
near-perfect expected workload does not certify the hitting forecast.

## Exposed 2026 examples

These have cutoff 2025 and target 2026. They use the already evaluated frozen
forecast, not a revised model. Their counts must not select a repair.

**Jesus Made.** Age eighteen, his 378 Single-A, 123 High-A and 24 Double-A
PA are all present. Primary exposure is Single A, 72% of his affiliated PA.
The .98 rank raises the exact tree paths. Probability is 33.7%, conditional PA
265 and expected PA 89 versus zero observed. Rate is −.17 wins per 600;
contribution .25 versus zero. Only six narrowly matching training people exist
and two arrived; no strict same-origin peer exists. A coarse upper-minors label
is potentially confusing, but the model did not lose his lower-level history.

**Leo De Vries.** Age eighteen, he has 433 High-A and 103 Double-A PA,
with rank score .97. Probability is 39.5%, conditional PA 306 and expected PA
121 versus zero actual; contribution .34 versus zero. Thirteen matching training
people include six arrivals. The strict peer rule finds Griffin, Clark and
De Paula, who receive 339, 206 and 42 PA against 187, 218 and thirteen predicted.
Their outcomes show both advancement and uncertainty within elite young
profiles. A downweight based only on De Vries' eventual non-arrival would also
harm some legitimate advancement forecasts.

**TJ Rumfield.** Age 25, his Triple-A exposure is 474 PA in 2024 and 587
in 2025, with fifteen and sixteen homers. His 2025 record includes 108
strikeouts and 69 walks. He is unlisted and not on the dated 40-man roster.
Probability is 25.3%, conditional PA 106 and expected PA 27 versus 631 actual;
rate −.90 and contribution .04 versus 5.04 are also low. The model receives
966.2 recency-weighted Triple-A PA, not an old DSL shortcut. The matching
training population is substantial, 759 people and 240 active people. Selected
peers Hickey, Murray and Franklin have zero, 33 and 59 actual PA; their roster,
position and production are not identical. This is a genuine missed player,
not proof that an omitted Triple-A record explains it.

**Kevin McGonigle.** Age twenty, he has 206 Double-A PA with twelve homers,
26 strikeouts and 33 walks, plus 171 High-A PA. His .99 rank is present.
Probability is 72.6%, conditional PA 251 and expected PA 182 versus 707 actual;
rate .22 and contribution .63 versus 4.70 are too low. Sixteen matching
training people include twelve active people. Only one strict same-origin
peer exists, Walcott, who receives zero against 73 predicted PA. This is a
substantial-experience, high-rank miss—not a twenty-four-PA promotion cameo.
An elite-prospect signal exists, but the forecast understates his eventual
workload and hitting breakout.

## What these walks establish

The exact inputs and head replays rule out a claimed missing level-PA or
missing-ranking explanation for these cases. They do not establish that a tree
path's negative zero-MLB-workload term is an inappropriate rule: first arrivals
have legitimately different workloads and the path attribution depends on
correlated variables. The previously tested prospect-only conditional head
already failed to improve this architecture. Repeating it is not a solution.

The review is complete, while predictive improvement and deployment approval
are not. Keep sparse support visible, restore actual input authority in future
audits, and require a new, falsifiable mechanism before another prospect model
test. Close entrant coverage and the public comparison next rather than using
these names to tune the already exposed final season.
