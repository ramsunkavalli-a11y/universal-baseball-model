# Next checkpoint: population audit, then one projection-anchored path test

2026-09-23. **Approved direction; not an executed experiment or release.**
This document tightens the next approach. It does not modify the frozen v1
experiment contract or its rejection. The present follow-up is documentation only.
See the [baseball simulation review](baseball-simulation-literature-2026-09-23.md).

## Objective and boundaries

Describe possible careers with our projection system, then value the transferable
rights to those careers. A better-looking histogram, a familiar prospect ranking
or an improved all-player average is not sufficient. Current projections, the
8777 explorer, protected 2026 outcomes and v1 artifacts remain untouched.

The next execution is bounded to a donor-population audit and one fixed population
repair. At most one projection-anchored architecture may follow if its feasibility
gate passes and a separate numeric contract is saved before fitting. No algorithm
tournament, threshold grid or tuning to Eldridge/public FV. Larger joint latent
state/survival models are deferred unless this bounded sequence diagnoses a need.

## Checkpoint A: audit support before examining new forecast outcomes

Compare the v1 latest-snapshot library with all eligible historical snapshots.
Retain the same target definitions, cutoff maturity, normal/pandemic split and
non-arrival/inactive/return states. Each donor's entire annual vector must mature
by the fitting cutoff. No unobserved future season becomes zero.

Produce one cutoff/horizon support inventory, recording:

- rows and distinct identities before and after latest-snapshot selection;
- age, level, prior MLB exposure, recent minor workload and time since promotion,
  where exact dated evidence is available; missing promotion timing stays unknown;
- counts and effective identity support for the fixed age/stage cells and the
  already exposed young brief-MLB subgroup (age <=23, 1–99 current MLB PA);
- which young snapshots are lost, without choosing players based on later success;
- split overlap, self-donor exclusions and the amount of borrowing across groups.

All snapshots of one identity must remain on the same side of a structure/donor
split. Identity balance is determined only from snapshots eligible at that cutoff,
not a player's eventual career length. The comparable population must not exclude
players merely because they never debuted or stopped playing. A minor-origin
donor is not automatically invalid for a brief MLB entrant; test conditional
support rather than imposing a player-specific ban.

Inventory feasible earlier origins and the availability of historical benchmark
forecasts without scoring new outcomes. Do not claim fresh confirmation for
previously inspected seasons, invent missing old forecasts or leak later fitted
coefficients into earlier origins. If support is inadequate, report that result
and stop the relevant scope; do not silently broaden matching until names look right.

## Checkpoint B: isolate population repair

Keep the rejected F1 recipe as the fixed control. A1 changes only the historical
sampling measure: retain all eligible snapshots and give each player total weight
one, divided across their eligible snapshots at that fit cutoff. Apply the same
measure to structure fitting and donor sampling. Preserve the v1 feature block,
forest settings and path targets; split by identity, never by individual row.

For conditional donor sampling, normalize those precomputed row weights within
the matching node. Record both unique identities and identity-level effective
sample size; many snapshots of one player are not many independent donors.
Save the precise node-support/fallback rule and effective-weight implementation
in the pre-fit execution manifest. Do not change age bins, features, tree capacity,
state definitions or calibration along with the population repair. Any necessary
software correction is separately documented and applied consistently to controls.

Before fitting, freeze input hashes, origin lists, random seeds, weights, support
rules, finite-draw score corrections and all acceptance calculations. Reproduce the
old control on matching rows first. A1 is an ablation, not a presumed winner.
Close it with an acceptance/rejection even if the proposed architecture cannot run.

## Checkpoint C: feasibility before one new architecture

Our inference from ZiPS/PECOTA is to retain a validated projection as the talent
anchor, while comparable changes or residual trajectories describe development
and dependence. Do not replace that anchor wholesale with absolute donor production.

Before fitting C1, certify:

1. Historical baseline skill/projection states can be reconstructed at each
   snapshot cutoff using only then-available predictors, coefficients and labels.
   Training-donor anchors need the same out-of-time discipline as outer forecasts.
2. Residual/development vectors, if used, are computed from those historical
   forecasts—not in-sample fitted residuals or today's model applied retrospectively.
3. The anchor's target is explicit. A skill-rate anchor and an unconditional WAR
   anchor are not interchangeable; never divide expected WAR by expected PA and
   call it talent. Define the bridge to opportunity rather than assuming it.
4. There is sufficient supported history for the intended horizon and states.
   State what is estimated individually versus regressed toward population change.

If any requirement fails, deliver the precise missing source/support and stop C1.
Do not fall back to a future-fitted anchor or forced post-hoc mean. If they pass,
freeze ONE mathematical specification, with its shrinkage, state transition,
workload, persistence and noise rules before scoring. No residual-versus-delta
selection based on the outer results. This architecture is our hypothesis, not
an implementation claimed to be ZiPS or PECOTA.

Within C1, opportunity/continuation should respond to simulated ability and the
prior known role/duration. Separate uncertainty about persistent ability from
genuine development, availability and season-event variation. Do not redraw a
new unrelated talent every season, multiply independent marginal averages, or
choose eventual career success using future labels. Do not add event noise already
contained in observed residual paths; an empirical realized-outcome distribution
must not be mislabeled a pure latent-talent distribution.

## Comparators, scores and safeguards to lock before fitting

Retain delivered mean projections, B0, F0 and rejected F1 from v1 on identical rows.
Compare A1 with F1 to isolate population effects. Compare C1, if feasible, with A1
and the stronger applicable references—not only the weakest age/stage benchmark.
A direct probability benchmark is a useful non-simulation diagnostic; inventory
and reuse the existing positive-tail/skill-tail results first. One additional
fixed direct benchmark requires an explicit pre-fit specification, not another
sweep over the same failed thresholds. Simulation is not required to win.

Keep H3 normal tests distinct from pandemic-affected H6. Do not stitch separate
horizon fits into a coherent path or treat pandemic-only H6 evidence as normal
confirmation. Preserve the v1 event definitions: no MLB PA; two 450-PA seasons;
six cumulative batting/replacement wins; two four-batting/replacement-win seasons.
These overlap and are not scouting grades or All-Star outcomes.

Carry forward the v1 numerical safeguards as the starting contract: >=3 normal
origins for a development-support claim, favorable paired CRPS evidence against
the relevant distribution controls, majority-origin improvement, <=5% mean-MSE
harm versus the delivered model, <=5% pooled event-score harm, and <=10% CRPS harm
in supported starting groups (>=200 rows and >=3 origins). Report both cumulative
CRPS and joint path scores, Brier/log loss, counts, interval widths and calibration.
The success checks retain >=30 observed positives, predicted/observed counts in
[0.75,1.25], and no worsening of Brier/log loss against the applicable stronger
predeclared comparator. Neither mostly-zero coverage nor a mean-score win overrides
failure in supported prospect-success checks.

Before scoring, explicitly assign these checks to never-debuted prospects, recent
debut players and the exposed young brief-MLB group, including unsupported status
where thresholds cannot be met. The 98-row v1 diagnostic is NOT a newly independent
test. Do not weaken the minimum support to force a pass or tune its probability
levels after seeing results. Additional cohorts, if recoverable, require their own
cutoff-safe references. Otherwise report bounded exploratory evidence, not solved
prospect upside. Public grades may only appear in a separately dated audit.

Keep whole-player cluster bootstrap and nonoverlapping-window checks, disclose
overlapping seasons, and keep the fully player-disjoint sensitivity. It cannot
be compared as equally disjoint to an inherited reference that is not.

Monte Carlo check: hold fitted models, identity splits and weights fixed. Repeat
only the draw stage with five predeclared seeds and 400 draws, plus a fixed 1,600-draw
stability run. Use identical scoring conventions for all methods, accounting for
finite-draw bias where applicable. If decision signs change with the draw seed,
report numerical instability and withhold promotion; never pick the best seed.
More draws reduce numerical noise, not cohort or opportunity bias.

## Deliverable and stopping point

The next executable milestone closes with a support audit, frozen A1 recipe,
matched result, code/tests and a C1 feasibility decision. A feasible C1 receives
one separately frozen test, not automatic adoption. A failed A1 or C1 stays failed;
do not blend, clip or rescale it after viewing the same outcomes. Existing live
means remain the default until an explicit complete acceptance decision.

Whole-WAR accounting, joint service/rights paths, sourced salary obligations and
the beyond-2031 control/liability tail still gate player dollar values. Keep the
tested valuation interface and never double-discount modeled failure risk.
Future entrants remain a separate league-cohort reserve, not assets currently
owned or a justification to inflate the current player pool. Preserve the
distinction between model surplus, market return and team-specific utility.
