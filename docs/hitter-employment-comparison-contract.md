## Scoring and interpretation

Primary loss is equal-origin original-cohort PA squared error. Report PA RMSE,
MAE and bias, Brier, log loss, fixed-talent delivered batting RMSE/MAE/bias,
participants and PA/value totals. Report every year, stage, previous-debut status,
current regular/partial, absent prior-debut, affected/unaffected source flags,
foreign rows and additions separately. Show allocation to eventual participants
and nonparticipants. Lower-minor deterioration and large total errors cannot be
hidden behind a pooled gain. Cohort sums are forecasts of this fixed population,
not totals for future unknown entrants or future organization membership.

Use 2,000 paired whole-player bootstrap repetitions, seed 84, with original
equal-origin weights. These are nominal development intervals after many exposed
tests, not independent confirmation. Show matched historical Steamer workload
and batting contribution on the existing qualified prior-MLB cohort. Keep
public release-date, park and rate-reference limitations; do not interpret
ordinary ZiPS PA as unconditional workload or absent foreign public rows as zero.
Public hitting-rate comparison does not change because talent is held fixed.

A defensible predictive improvement requires a negative primary loss change
with interval below zero, no material (>2%) PA-RMSE deterioration in a populated
major stage, and no systematic worsening of origin/cohort totals or batting
contribution. Inspect sparse groups and individual cases regardless of sign.
These are working decision checks, not a declaration of acceptable universal
error or automatic deployment. A valid data correction is retained even if its
refitted learner loses; that does not establish a better projection.
