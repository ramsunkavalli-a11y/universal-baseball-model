# Identify nonbatting talent before forecasting annual contribution

2026-10-06. The primary component question is whether available evidence
identifies defensive and running ability that carries to MLB. How many runs a
player contributes next year is a later question combining ability, development,
arrival, position and opportunities. This correction supersedes the next-test
ordering in the October 5 nonbatting review without altering its sealed results.

## Why the last test was not the right primary test

The infield play-share experiment primarily predicted next-year delivered MLB
range runs. Its secondary quality test still required substantial MLB infield
time the very next year. It consequently had no measured quality outcomes for
the recent A/rookie cohorts and little support for young AA players.

That was a legitimate near-term contribution experiment but an inadequate
primary test of minor-league defensive talent. Zero runs from not reaching MLB
does not mean zero ability. Nor does failure to improve next-year contribution
prove a minor defensive indicator is useless. The candidate remains unapproved
for production; its eventual-talent value is unresolved, not rejected.

## Three distinct predictions

1. **Ability and development:** fielding/running quality at a stated position
   and age, with uncertainty. Separate range, arms, double plays, catcher
   blocking/throwing/framing, and running decisions/success. Position itself is
   a role/transition prediction, not a fielding-quality score.
2. **Opportunity:** whether the player reaches/stays in MLB, which positions he
   occupies and how much exposure he receives. Useful defenders can fail to
   reach MLB because of hitting; excellent runners can lack playing time.
3. **Contribution/value:** combine the distributions of ability and opportunity,
   including their dependence, into annual and longer-term runs/WAR/value.

A talent input should first be judged on question 1. Question 3 is an integration
check, not the sole admission test. This does not mean any repeatable minor stat
is MLB talent, or that adding a correlated component must improve total value.

## What the talent test needs

Use later MLB **quality per defensive or running opportunity**, not next-year
total runs, as the main external check. Pool observations across a predeclared
future window to reduce single-season noise. The source inventory must determine
whether position-specific native MLB labels are available: the last experiment's
player-aggregate range target can mix infield and outfield performance.

Define forecast age/position and future measurement window before fitting. Allow
time for lower-level prospects to develop, while retaining age, elapsed time,
position changes and opportunity uncertainty. Later performance is evidence of
developed ability, not an exact observation of what the player could do at the
original cutoff. Do not choose the player's best season, wait selectively until
his rating looks convincing or mix incomparable measurement definitions.

Keep every origin-eligible player in the prediction/coverage ledger. Non-arrivals
have unknown MLB quality, not an average/poor-defense label. They still matter
to arrival and delivered-value testing. Players with measured MLB quality are a
selected subset: report how their origin profiles differ from non-arrivals,
and limit transport claims accordingly. Shrink noisy observations and propagate
their measurement uncertainty; one short MLB cameo is not an elite talent grade.

Supportive checks can examine adjusted minor performance, movers between levels
and independent tracking/scouting where legitimately available. These are
measurement/development checks, not replacements for future-MLB confirmation.
Use unsuccessful origin-known peers and role-switchers in the player review.

## Immediate execution order

First inventory **joint source and target support**: minor-origin evidence,
position-specific MLB measurements, follow-up length, distinct people and actual
chronological training examples. Run this before population-scale catcher
collection or another fit. Existing source certification is retained; it does
not settle what outcome the next learner should predict.

For each proposed age/level/position window, show which old cohorts have complete
follow-up and which labels were already mature at the forecasting cutoff. A
2016-onward event file is not automatically sufficient to validate five-year
DSL talent forecasts chronologically. Historical published results are development
evidence; simply exposing longer follow-up does not create a fresh holdout.

Then lock ONE component-specific talent contract with age/position/simple
traditional-stat baselines, exposure-based measurement and shrinkage. Choose
the first component by usable support, not whichever later outcome looks best.
Use tracking only on a matched covered comparison and preserve all-level fallbacks.
If older/native quality labels are insufficient, record the precise gap and
needed source rather than return to next-year totals and rename them talent.

After a valid talent comparison and mandatory player walkthrough, check whether
the signal improves the unchanged opportunity/value system or is being lost in
aggregation. A useful talent result and a weak delivered-value result need
different diagnoses. Do not compensate by tuning to Peña, Mayer or another miss.

No new model was fitted for this correction. Existing contracts, models, forecasts,
completed 2026 evaluation and explorer are unchanged. The corrected target and
support audit are the next gate; the full nonbatting/value goal remains unfinished.
