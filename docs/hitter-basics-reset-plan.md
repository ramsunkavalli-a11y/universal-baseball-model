## What we are trying to accomplish

Build a useful forecast from readily obtainable baseball information: how well
a player will hit in MLB, how much he will play, and the resulting contribution.
Then extend that foundation to other skills, longer careers and ownership value.
Those later questions are not answered by next-year batting alone.

The reset is complete when the selected construction is understandable, the
main successful and unsuccessful experiments have a defensible interpretation,
its complexity has a basic competence check, and one justified next repair has
been chosen. Completion of this reset is not completion of the player-value model.

## Start with baseball, not a software library

1. Past production must survive sensible regression, recency and level translation.
   More reliable new MLB evidence should gradually outweigh older minor evidence.
   A brief debut must not automatically establish a completely new talent identity.
2. Being able to hit, being likely to reach MLB, and getting a full season are
   different things. A known temporary absence is not evidence of permanent exit.
3. A good team/league total can conceal giving the right PA to the wrong players.
   A good value total can conceal opposite hitting and workload errors.
4. Prospects can have valuable upside while their expected immediate MLB total is
   small. Immediate zero PA does not prove a DSL player's ability estimate correct.
5. Common public projections are competence checks, not a license to compare
   different dates, metrics, populations or conditional/unconditional workloads.

## Why the public models are the right smell test

[Tango's original Marcel description](https://www.tangotiger.net/archives/stud0346.shtml)
shows that recency, regression and age provide a transparent starting point.
It does not translate every minor leaguer or allocate every future job.
[FanGraphs' projection explanation](https://library.fangraphs.com/principles/projections/)
separates performance estimation from depth-chart workload.
[Steamer's 2015 release](https://blogs.fangraphs.com/instagraphs/2015-steamer-projections/)
explicitly used depth-chart playing time, while Steamer600 held exposure fixed.
[Szymborski's explanation](https://blogs.fangraphs.com/dan-szymborski-fangraphs-chat-1-7-19/)
distinguishes ZiPS performance potential from forecasting a particular team's
decision to give a prospect an MLB job. These sources explain the comparison
problem; they do not establish the exact information dates of our 2022–25 files.

AI assistance is not extra baseball evidence. Better historical coverage, sound
translation, sensible shrinkage and knowing likely jobs can beat a more complicated
model built with weaker assumptions. Our measured public gap is mainly workload;
there is no established blanket claim that ZiPS/Steamer beat this entire model 20%.
