# Where prospect playing time forecasts fall short

2026-10-05. This source-aligned diagnosis does not change a forecast or fit a
model. It closes the proposed promotion-cameo explanation as a demonstrated
missing-input bug. The current model receives separate level exposure. Its
remaining prospect errors involve probability, workload and hitting, with
sparse examples for unusual elite profiles. There is no single justified
prospect multiplier.

## What was checked

All 2,827,104 annual level-PA fields reconcile against source counts: three
seasons and fourteen level buckets for 63,282 historical input rows and 4,030
frozen 2026 forecasts. All 42 level-PA fields are among the 251 playing-time
inputs. Neither the explorer's coarse stage nor its snapshot-level label is
used directly by those heads. Thus calling Made an upper-minors player does
not erase his 378 Single-A PA, 123 High-A PA and 24 Double-A PA.

Highest level means any positive origin-year PA. Primary level means the most
PA, with a higher-level tie break. The broad diagnosis combines the several
rookie buckets but preserves DSL and each raw rookie source in the walks.
Mexico remains separate. These annual counts do not reveal the date of a
promotion; the file called dated stints contains annual team records, not
dated game stints. A timing feature would need another source.

Two interrupted audit executions are preserved. First the prediction export
lacked fitted game columns. Then six inherited export columns differed from
the actual fitted inputs, including old ranking and fractional exposure
evidence. The [execution note](hitter-prospect-exposure-audit-execution-note.md)
records exact differences. The completed audit joins actual input rows, retains
legacy evidence separately and replays both PA heads. It does not treat old
baseline columns as the current model's input or reinterpret missing columns
as zero. Historical rank groups now use the actual preseason ranks.

## Historical errors are not concentrated in promotion cameos

The unchanged forecasts cover 24,199 never-debut player-seasons, representing
10,076 distinct people at seven origins. Non-arrivals remain in every primary
total. These are exposed development years, not new independent tests.

| Origin-known group | Records | Forecast MLB PA | Actual MLB PA | Forecast arrivals | Actual arrivals |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| All never-debut records | 24,199 | 81,849 | 98,328 | 677.0 | 787 |
| Highest level has 1–29 PA | 2,957 | 5,601 | 6,203 | 48.1 | 51 |
| Highest level has 30–199 PA | 12,813 | 28,721 | 31,350 | 223.2 | 233 |
| Highest level has at least 200 PA | 6,977 | 46,829 | 60,532 | 397.7 | 500 |
| No current affiliated PA | 1,452 | 699 | 243 | 8.1 | 3 |

The 200-plus group accounts for 13,703 of the 16,479 missing PA, roughly 83%.
It is short in six of seven origins but overpredicts in origin 2023. There is
evidence of a recurring readiness/allocation problem, not evidence that more
PA should be assigned to every prospect. The small-sample group has a much
smaller aggregate shortage. Records can contain the same person in different
years; these counts are not independent sample sizes.

Highest and primary levels differ in 4,421 historical records. That group is
short 3,545 PA but **overpredicts** batting contribution, 90.84 versus 80.63
custom wins. Fixing a PA total alone does not guarantee improved player value.
All errors here use the existing common-origin batting-plus-replacement ledger,
with the unchanged translated prospect hitting rate, not full WAR.

Among the actual 787 debutants, the conditional-workload predictions sum to
98,088 PA versus 98,328 actual—nearly right in aggregate. Expected PA is lower
because their uncertainty discount subtracts 54,656 PA, while predictions for
the 23,412 non-arrival records add back 38,417 PA. The exact identity is
−240 − 54,656 + 38,417 = −16,479 PA. It describes allocation after observing
outcomes; it is not a causal decomposition or permission to predict arrival
with hindsight. Correct totals can conceal wrong individual workloads.

All reported diagnostic losses use pooled, equally weighted records. They
must not be compared as new improvements against earlier equal-origin-year
headlines. No model, forecast or loss weighting was changed by this audit.

## The reviewed 2026 result is separate

The fixed frozen cohort has 3,116 never-debut players. It predicts 102.7 arrivals
versus 105 observed but 13,098 PA versus 15,976. For actual arrivals, conditional
PA totals are 14,058 versus 15,976. Here both conditional workloads and their
allocation are short; history's nearly correct conditional aggregate does not
carry over perfectly.

The 200-plus group gets 7,546 PA versus 9,760 actual. The 1–29 group instead
gets 631 versus 572: it is not uniformly underpredicted. The top-20 prospect
group gets 2,201 versus 3,418 PA, but contains just thirteen players. These
2026 splits explain the already completed evaluation; they do not authorize
choosing a repair against this final season. Its future-season-relative value
ledger is kept separate from the historical common-origin ledger.

## Player checks and their implications

Fourteen focal players and twenty-five outcome-blind peers have exact saved
PA-head replays. The machine-readable walks contain all 251 actual inputs,
three-season source statistics, annual team records, tree paths, distinct-person
training support and intermediate forecasts. The [player review](hitter-prospect-exposure-allocation-player-review.md)
explains all fourteen cases, including contradictory examples.

Kurtz and Langford are not missing their rankings. Kurtz has only fifty
professional PA and no active training person matching the narrow ranked
profile; Langford also has none. Julio and Alonso have meaningful upper-level
samples but remain too low. Holliday and Meadows go the opposite way. Kwan is
underpredicted despite strong contact and upper-minors evidence being present.
Rumfield is not a cameo case at all: two substantial Triple-A seasons are
present. Made and De Vries demonstrate why raising all elite teenagers would
also allocate more immediate MLB PA to non-arrivals.

## Decision and next work

Do not rerun the already failed prospect-only conditional-head recipe or the
already tested primary-level/COVID ideas under new names. Do not add a primary
level field simply because the explorer label appears too coarse. Source
integrity passed; conditional aggregation alone did not establish individual
accuracy. Attribution paths show numerical use, not causal feature importance.

The highest-priority practical work is now source coverage for foreign entrants
and a genuinely comparable public benchmark. Preserve this evaluated candidate
as the research reference. Before another prospect fit, require a specific
mechanism not already tested, actual ranked-profile training support and a
historical contrast that retains both missed arrivals and false high forecasts.
The simple cases reviewed here do not justify a universal boost or a claimed
predictive gain. The long-range goal remains active; this is not full WAR,
six years of club control, trade value or deployment approval.
