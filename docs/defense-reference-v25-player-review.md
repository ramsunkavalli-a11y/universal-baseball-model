# Player examples of defensive references and sparse priors

Eight fixed 2022 origins and their three origin-selected peers are traced from
dated observations into saved forecasts and actual 2023 position exposure. No
new prediction was made. Measured raw versus position-relative range can be
compared; naive conversion of a shrunk forecast remains a diagnostic, not a
validated talent upgrade. All other channels, batting and opportunities remain
available in the machine-readable walks.

## Center and corner fielders

Trout's 2020–2022 CF observations are 1,363, 885 and 2,813 native outs, with
−1.112, +0.938 and +2.686 range runs. Recency weighting supplies 3,596.25 outs;
the 3,000-out prior leaves 54.52% reliability and +0.654 raw runs per 500 innings.
His held-person cutoff reference is +1.896. The own-repertoire projected exposure
therefore carries +3.457 baseline runs. That is about 0.346 custom wins in the
value definition, not an accuracy gain or necessarily a correct new grade.
His actual 2023 CF record is +3.561 raw range runs over 2,043 outs; +2.726 is
the measured position reference, leaving +0.835 relative-to-CF range runs.
Arm credit remains separate.

Trout's peers are Enrique Hernández, Odúbel Herrera and Adam Engel. Hernández
subsequently plays mostly infield, so his projected CF reference offset +2.995
becomes only +0.395 across his actual outfield mix. This is a role forecast issue,
not proof that CF talent disappeared. Herrera has no actual MLB field exposure;
delivered credit is zero while underlying quality remains unobserved. Engel's
three LF outs lack measured range, preserving an unknown complete target rather
than filling it with average defense.

Happ's recent LF history supplies 4,434.5 weighted outs and 59.65% reliability.
His saved raw history rate is +0.354; his LF reference is −1.179. Projected LF,
CF and RF exposure together carry −1.924 reference runs. His actual LF range
is −6.546 raw runs over 4,015 outs, versus −2.848 after the measured LF reference.
He is still below the measured LF average; changing units is not a turnaround.
Peers Arozarena and Benintendi also remain negative in actual LF range after
centering. O'Neill's LF is positive in both definitions; his separate CF record
is slightly positive raw and negative relative to CF. Positions cannot be pooled
just because all are labeled outfield.

Soto's saved RF history rate is −1.644 after 5,766.25 weighted outs and 65.78%
reliability. Subtracting the RF reference leaves −0.910 in the diagnostic
conversion, still negative. In 2023 he plays LF: −6.229 raw range runs become
−2.513 relative to LF over 4,035 outs. The saved RF forecast cannot explain that
role change. His peers Cal Mitchell and Oswaldo Cabrera retain missing small
stints; neither gets a fabricated complete outcome. Andrew Vaughn moves from
poor outfield records to first base, where this OF reference correction is zero.
That move requires a role forecast, not adjustment of his old RF quality.

Bellinger carries +2.863 projected reference runs, mostly CF, but his 2023 role
includes 1,265 first-base outs and 2,058 CF outs. CF range +1.747 raw becomes
−0.999 relative to CF, while first-base range and receiving stay distinct and
unchanged. His peers Lane Thomas, Jose Siri and Jake Meyers expose both position
change and genuine positive range. Thomas moves largely to RF. Siri's CF range
remains strongly positive, +8.806 raw and +5.465 position-relative; Meyers's
CF +6.533 becomes +3.317. The reference change does not erase good defense.

Castellanos's recent RF history has 5,062.75 weighted outs and 62.79% reliability,
with a −2.402 raw history rate. The cutoff RF reference is −0.939, leaving a
−1.463 diagnostic rate. Actual RF range −4.619 becomes −2.565 over 3,877 outs;
his arm credit is separate and also affects total defense. Hunter Renfroe,
Randal Grichuk and Hunter Dozier are the peers. Grichuk's later LF exposure has
no corresponding recent MLB LF range history; that stays unknown evidence.
Dozier's missing 15-out LF measurement prevents a fabricated complete target.

## Unknown prospect talent must not become a negative center field grade

Rafaela has no native MLB range history at the origin. Original minor splits
retain 5,478 fielding outs across 2021–2022, with position and level detail; none
is silently converted into native MLB range. His own-repertoire projected OF
exposure carries +0.621 reference runs. Subtracting it from the legacy raw-zero
fallback would impose below-average CF quality without evidence. A neutral
position-relative prior is the correct explicit fallback mean for that value
definition, while talent itself stays unknown.

The peers are Johan Rojas, Jorge Barrosa and Diego Hernandez. Rojas has 5,453
minor fielding outs and later 1,176 CF outs with +4.377 raw or +2.808 relative
range runs. Rafaela has 350 later CF outs with +1.426 raw or +0.959 relative
credit, plus separate SS and 2B exposure. Those are brief later observations,
not reliable peak-talent labels. Barrosa and Hernandez have no 2023 MLB field
exposure, which is zero delivered value but unknown MLB fielding ability.
The similar minor usage does not establish identical ability or arrival odds.

The sparse-prior issue also affects lightly observed MLB positions. Hernández's
24 weighted LF outs yield only 0.79% reliability. After shrinking raw range
toward zero, subtracting the full LF reference yields +0.914 relative runs per
500 innings. That artificial confidence comes from mismatched prior/reference
coordinates. Center the evidence before regression to position-neutral skill;
do not present this naive diagnostic conversion as a corrected projection.

## Infield and catcher controls

Witt's own-repertoire forecast and actual outcomes have no OF reference offset.
The saved total defense forecast −3.718 versus +9.756 actual remains a real
young-development miss. His peers Perdomo, Luis García Jr. and Livan Soto retain
their SS/2B/3B paths and differing sample sizes. This accounting correction does
not solve their position changes or talent development.

Willson Contreras also has zero OF offset in the own-repertoire arm. His forecast
defense +0.354 versus −4.114 actual is unchanged. His 2,292 actual catcher outs
and 30 DH starts remain separate. Peers Omar Narváez, Christian Bethancourt and
Andrew Knapp retain catcher/first-base history and exits. Catcher range is not
a measured zero: its distinct framing, throwing and blocking channels are used.
Knapp's small nonzero projected LF allocation remains a role-model artifact,
not proof of LF talent or a reason to change catcher fielding.

## Review decision

The accounting mismatch is real, but does not explain every miss or disprove
the previously useful intrinsic range baseline. Some individual credit changes
are material even when totals cancel. The source/prior mismatch must be repaired
before a position-relative value test. All fixed cases, peers, raw minor splits,
annual native records and saved channel arithmetic independently replay. Retain
intrinsic quality and explicit unknown talent; no automatic deployment follows.
