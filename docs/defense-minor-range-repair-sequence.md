# Repair sample handling before using minor fielding grades

The latest minor-range review identifies specific failures, not a reason to
start another model-family search. Keep the actual MLB-history quality baseline
and repair the way minor counts become evidence. Sparse prospect estimates must
describe uncertainty; near-zero MLB opportunity must not hide bad talent grades.

## Source reliability comes first

The moment-based recipe can erase substantial CF samples while giving six
error-free complex chances a 94.5% weight. First compare its count-distribution
calibration with a count-likelihood approach, without fitting future MLB quality.
Use existing chronological source counts only, held people excluded everywhere.
The exact source contract, population, score and uncertainty checks must be saved
before estimation. Retain zero-count and short-exposure records; do not delete
uncomfortable examples or manually floor reliability to make them look sensible.

For errors, a beta-binomial likelihood accounts for the actual trial counts. A
one-chance observation has success probability equal to the prior mean, regardless
of prior concentration. This follows directly from the
[documented beta-binomial mass function](https://mc-stan.org/docs/2_39/functions-reference/bounded_discrete_distributions.html#beta-binomial-distribution).
It is a mathematical reason to reconsider the shortcut, not proof that a fitted
likelihood model will identify MLB defenders. People with multiple source years
need a declared shared-rate/development treatment; rows must not masquerade as
independent players. Context variation remains distinct from persistent talent.

Measure how well source distributions predict later count observations, with
proper likelihood/calibration checks, as an input-calibration gate only. Passing
that gate cannot substitute for prediction of future MLB quality. Replay all
small-sample reference examples and their origin-selected peers, including the
one-chance records behind Canzone's reference and Frick's unfamiliar thin 3B stint.
Use training-only support for fallback; report unsupported contrast ranges across
all forecasts, not just the few eventual MLB survivors that can be scored.

## Preserve the useful baseline while testing added information

After the source gate and mandatory walkthrough, lock a separate comparison:
hold the existing age/position/level baseline fixed and learn a separately
regularized count correction using mature chronological, player-separated
training residuals. Training residual generation must avoid in-sample optimism
and held-player contamination. Inner tuning must retain enough actual measured
people and expose missing support. The unchanged baseline is the fallback when
the correction cannot be supported; an extrapolation flag alone is insufficient.

Compare the old selected recipe, unchanged baseline and corrected augmentation
on matched identities. Keep 2022 and 2021 separate and label these now-exposed
cohorts as development evidence. Do not promote a better-looking fixed penalty
from the preceding diagnostic. No 2026 outcomes, forecast changes or explorer
promotion are part of this repair. If corrected counts do not carry useful MLB
quality information, keep the fallback and move to the next identified defense
gap; do not indefinitely retune the same population.

## What the broader defense layer still needs

The quality layer must eventually combine supported MLB range history, credible
minor evidence, throwing, first-base receiving, double plays and catcher skill.
Position usage and movement are separate from quality; Turang, Edwards and
Rafaela illustrate why one position's outcome cannot certify another. Catcher
transfer needs additional comparable historical MLB quality support rather than
relaxed outcome cutoffs. Lower-minors trajectories need longer-path mature labels
or explicit uncertainty, not survivor-only certification of every DSL player.

Only after a quality component survives should its contribution to delivered
value be checked with arrival and position exposure handled separately. Actual
future innings may explain a score but cannot be supplied as forecast workload.
Full WAR and years of control remain distinct from three calendar years of
conditional range quality. The frozen forecast remains unchanged throughout.

The older [Bayesball fielding study](https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.4317) combines spatial
opportunity information with hierarchical sharing among fielders at each position.
Its lesson is to distinguish play difficulty from player skill and share evidence
for sparse fielders. Its high-resolution batted-ball-location data do not establish
that our all-level counts contain equivalent information; copying its method name
would not close this gap.
