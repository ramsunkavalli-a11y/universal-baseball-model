# Nonbatting hitter value milestone

2026-10-05. Current user-directed goal: settle a defensible starting recipe for
running, position, general fielding and catching, and test a genuinely different
minor-league defensive signal against future MLB outcomes. The broader hitter
goal is unfinished and paused in the app; this repository milestone records the
new work without falsely completing or replacing it.

## What counts as completion

Reconcile the old frozen packages with the newer component research. Record for
each component what it measures, coverage, future-MLB evidence, applicable
horizons and remaining limitations. Preserve useful recipes rather than repeat
their original searches. Complete one bounded measurement/transport comparison
and its player walkthrough before choosing more experiments. A negative or
unsupported result closes that recipe, not the existence of defensive talent.
This milestone does not automatically finish full WAR or six years of control.

Batting, playing time, frozen 2026 evaluation and the explorer stay unchanged.
No additional 2026 outcome access. No team-record testing, college collection,
algorithm tournament, personal player override or post-result threshold search.

## Literature and existing evidence

Sean Smith's [2008 Total Zone method](https://tht.fangraphs.com/measuring-defense-for-players-back-to-1956/)
estimates opportunities as well as plays made; it separates range from arms,
double plays and receiving. [His revision](https://tht.fangraphs.com/measuring-defense-for-players-back-to-1956-part-2/)
addresses handedness, pitcher mix, occupied bases, park changes and missing
plays. It also reports that extra plausible adjustments did not clearly improve
agreement with other measures. This is a measurement recipe, not proof of
future minor-to-MLB transport. UBM's first-touch-selected infield denominator
omitted many through hits. The complete ground-ball ledger repairs accounting,
but arbitrary adjacent-fielder shares are not certified individual range.

[Bayesball](https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.4317) shares information among fielders
at a position using spatial data. Its measurement setting does not establish
that our public coordinates are comparable interception locations or that a
minor-league spatial rating forecasts MLB performance. Coarse observable
features and heavy shrinkage are a defensible fallback, not substitute tracking.

[Lichtman's UZR primer](https://blogs.fangraphs.com/the-fangraphs-uzr-primer/)
emphasizes context and multi-year evidence. The established running and MLB
defensive histories remain useful; the old minor range persistence tests do not
by themselves settle translation. Position usage, range and playing time must
not receive overlapping credit.

[Carruth and Jensen](https://arxiv.org/abs/0705.3257) and
[Marchi's minor-league arms study](https://tht.fangraphs.com/cannons-in-the-bushes/)
model advancement opportunities, including holds and outs, rather than assists
alone. Marchi explicitly did not establish a park-adjusted, regressed MLB
projection. UBM already has running/runner-context work; an outfielder-arm
extension needs a separately certified opportunity/attribution ledger before
being called new player value.

Official [OAA](https://www.mlb.com/glossary/statcast/outs-above-average)
uses fielders' movement, available time and starting positions. Public batted-ball
tracking alone does not recreate those measurements. UBM's tracked AAA/FSL source
transport worked, but its original tracked-MiLB transfer gate had zero eligible
players. That is insufficient evidence, not a demonstrated negative effect of
tracking. Use the existing MLB tracked recipe only within its certified scope;
do not project proprietary-quality MiLB OAA from available EV/LA columns alone.

## One new range question

Can an infielder's share of successfully handled ground balls, measured against
ALL ground balls while he was present, predict future MLB range better than a
coarse age/level/position benchmark and the old first-touch-selected proxy?

Unlike the old proxy, through hits stay in the denominator without deciding
which adjacent infielder deserved the blame. The recorded first handler is an
outcome credit, not a pre-play opportunity filter or difficulty covariate. A
single ground ball supplies at most one successful credit across positions;
shared lineup exposure is not nine independent defensive plays. Estimate expected
credits from other defenses in the same league/handedness/park, then regress
player residuals heavily. This is a coarse play-share proxy with unresolved
pitcher/positioning effects, not an OAA reconstruction.

The separate pre-fit contract must lock source eligibility, future-MLB target,
support, fixed comparisons, loss/coverage checks and player case rules. If the
source/support check blocks a useful fit, record that rather than score unknown
defense as zero. Lower-level ratings cannot become precise MLB talent estimates
merely because few lower-level players reach MLB.

## Delivery order

1. Freeze the contract and source checks; build the two coarse measurements on
   the same complete ground-ball ledger, retaining unresolved records separately.
2. Audit actual chronological/player-held-out MLB target support and retain every
   eligible starting player in the forecast ledger. Distinguish conditional quality
   from delivered component contribution and unobserved quality on non-arrival.
3. Run the bounded comparison only where support permits; preserve neutral and
   simple baselines. Walk gains, harms, false highs/lows and ordinary players.
4. Record the component decisions, unresolved exposure/integration limits and
   concrete next gate. Commit and push the completed milestone. No automatic
   deployment or rerun of already closed algorithms/weights follows.
