How close is this to public projections?

Same 1,789 forecasts for active-at-cutoff hitters, including players with no MLB PA next year. Each forecast year has equal weight. The baseline's PA RMSE is about 7% worse than Steamer, and its average absolute PA error about 20% worse. The playing-time goal is not finished. Public archives can contain later roster/injury information; converted batting value has a run-environment difference, so a lower value error here does not establish better hitting talent.

What information does the model use, and what is missing?

The baseline uses three years of batting counts separately by league, age, observed career/debut history, listed position, dated draft evidence and an incomplete year-end roster listing. Recent production gets more weight; tiny samples are pulled toward fixed priors. MLB and minor-league competition are not treated as equal.

The games challenger adds actual games played and PA per game. Those show use, not diagnosed health or guaranteed future jobs. The contact challengers add ground/line/fly/pop-up direction profiles with exposure and source flags; these are not fully park-neutralized or tracking-based quality measurements. Uncorroborated PBP rows are excluded from those inputs, not from the player population.

Important remaining weaknesses: fast major-league arrivals, high-pedigree players with little professional data, returns after absences, uncertain future health/jobs, unobserved foreign production, and coherent long-term uncertainty. Team filtering uses the historical organization, not the player's later destination.

No 2026 outcomes are loaded. All compared experiments have completed player reviews; a failed challenger is shown for transparency, not as an endorsed forecast.
