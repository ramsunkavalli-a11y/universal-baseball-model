What changed, and how much to trust it

Years 1–3 retain the existing batting, playing-time and component forecasts exactly. Years 4–6 use separate future-year ridge forecasts based on age, level, ordinary batting results, and three years of performance/workload history. Players who never arrive in MLB remain in the test population. The later-year PA hurdle is refitted separately, not inferred from batting value.

The later-year component scenario uses dated position transitions and the existing regressed running/MLB-defense benchmarks. It is not an independently validated full-WAR forecast. General defense is poorly informed for players without MLB history. Batting and PA are separate estimates; their ratio is not a validated measure of hitting talent.

The zero-framing option is a sensitivity test, not a prediction of when full ABS will arrive. Position, PA, and defense are not yet jointly reconciled. No 2026 results were used and no original forecast was overwritten. Prediction ranges remain unavailable.

Control requires service-time paths, not multiplying expected PA by a service conversion. Injured MLB players can accrue service without batting. Reaching six service years generally creates eligibility after that season; a contract can extend beyond that, while a non-tender can end club control sooner. Current contracts and organization retention are not modeled here.
